Dr. Abdul Qadeer Khan

The father of Pakistan's nuclear programme - a scientist who made a nation stand tall.

Biography

Born in Bhopal in 1936, Abdul Qadeer Khan grew up in a family that valued education. After the partition of the subcontinent in 1947, his family migrated to Pakistan, settling in Karachi. He completed his early education there before travelling to Europe, where he earned a degree in metallurgical engineering from the University of Karachi and later pursued advanced studies in Berlin and Delft .

In 1972, while working at a uranium enrichment facility in the Netherlands, Khan came across classified documents describing a centrifuge-based method for enriching uranium. He studied the design deeply, then returned to Pakistan in 1976 at the request of Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, who famously said Pakistan would eat grass but would never allow itself to be threatened by nuclear blackmail .

Khan established the Engineering Research Laboratories (later renamed Kahuta Research Laboratories) and led a team that achieved uranium enrichment indigenously. On 28 May 1998, Pakistan successfully tested five nuclear devices in Chagai, Balochistan — becoming the first Muslim-majority nuclear state. Khan was hailed across Pakistan as the father of the nuclear programme .

He remained a controversial figure internationally, facing sanctions and later a government inquiry in 2004. Yet within Pakistan, he is remembered as a national hero who gave the country strategic parity. He spent his final years in Islamabad, writing and meeting students, and died on 10 October 2021 after a long illness. He was given a state funeral attended by thousands .
